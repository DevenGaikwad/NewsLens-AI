City engineers opened a new solar-powered water treatment pilot on Tuesday after an eighteen-month testing programme. The municipal laboratory reported that the unit processed forty thousand litres during its final trial while meeting the state limits for turbidity and bacterial contamination. Independent inspectors from the regional water board observed the test and published the measurement protocol on the board website. The pilot uses roof-mounted photovoltaic panels, a battery, membrane filters and ultraviolet disinfection. Officials said the project cost 2.4 million rupees, including monitoring equipment and two years of maintenance. The city council approved the expenditure in a recorded vote last September. Researchers cautioned that the pilot serves only one neighbourhood and should not be treated as proof that the same design will work at every site. A public review will compare energy use, maintenance costs and water-quality results after six months. Residents can view weekly readings at the treatment centre and submit questions during the review period.
